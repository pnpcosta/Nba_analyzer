# Problems Found

## 1. Misleading Shooting Percentages

A player who isn't a great three-point shooter but only takes shots when he is wide open might have a higher three-point percentage than a great shooter who is constantly guarded by the opponent's best defender.

To address this issue, we need to consider not only shooting efficiency but also shooting volume and shot difficulty.

One possible approach is to use an adjusted shooting percentage to reduce the impact of small sample sizes, while also considering the number of shots attempted.

## 2. Defensive Ratings

Steals and blocks alone do not fully represent a player's defensive abilities.

A player with more steals or blocks might frequently leave opponents open while trying to make defensive plays. Meanwhile, another player might have fewer steals or blocks because he is constantly guarding the opposing team's best offensive player.

To address this issue, we need to consider additional defensive metrics that better reflect a player's overall defensive impact, rather than relying only on steals and blocks.

## 3. Quality of 3 pointers

A player like Stephen Curry will shoot way more contested 3 pointers than a catch and shoot specialist, so we will have to take volume more in account.
